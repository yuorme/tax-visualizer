1
Your Worship and Members of City Council;
Today, as we prepare to unfold the City’s 2020/21 Operations Budget, we realize
that we are in times like no other where there is concern not only for the physical
and mental health of our residents, but also for their financial health and well-
being. One thing is clear, COVID-19 has changed how we interact with each
other, here at home and around the world.
As we look around our City, we see empty parking lots, streets, storefronts,
malls, office buildings, schools, restaurants, and places of worship. We see our
residents confined to their homes and neighbourhoods. We see the struggles of
those who are unemployed and without income, and know there are families
struggling to keep food on the table and to pay the mortgage or rent. We see it.
These are, and will be, challenging times.
But, what we also see, are glimmers of hope and the desire for neighbour to help
neighbour. I encourage you to continue to stay in touch; reach out by phone,
email, FaceTime, or other virtual means or, keeping your social distancing, wave
to let people know that you are thinking about them and that you do care.

Overall Synopsis…
Your Worship, even in these times, we continue to challenge our CAO and our
Senior Management Team to refine our operational costs and find greater
efficiencies. Certainly, in these times of challenge and change, those tasks are
magnified.
The City’s Budget is a guide which outlines our services levels, while at the same
time it identifies how the City will fund those services.
As a municipal government we are constantly facing a balancing act of trying to
meet the public expectation of maintaining our current service levels and looking
for opportunities to enhance those services.
Add to that balancing act, the need to maintain
and replace our aging infrastructure while, at
the same time, service our growth areas and
do it all in fiscally and operationally sustainable
way.
Unfortunately, in the months ahead, due to the
ongoing pandemic, the City will continue to
face budgetary pressures and revenue
challenges.
As we have done in the past, the City will, of course, manage its operations and
continue to work with Provincial and Federal governments, to ensure we strive to
meet the collective needs of our citizens and mitigate the fallout of the COVID-19
crisis.

Forging Ahead…
On January 28th of this year, Council held its pre-budget Open House at City Hall,
which provided an opportunity for our residents to once again meet with Council
and the City’s Senior Management Team to discuss and review any matter of
operational or budgetary concern. This year, we were fortunate to have more
than 50 residents drop by, share their views and provide input, which we
appreciated.
The City also received online input and met with various stakeholders within the
business community and the community at large to ensure a well balanced
perspective.
This year, Your Worship, we have been able to avoid a tax increase for both the
residential and commercial sectors keeping our $59.7 million operational budget
balanced. Water and Sewer Utility rates will increase by the Consumer Price
Index (CPI), at a rate of 1.2 per cent, as announced in last year’s budget
address. This will go towards balancing the Utilities $14.3 million operations
budget.
Council originally
approved its
2020/21 Capital
Budget with a gross
expenditure of $46
million; $30.5 million
for the City and
$15.5 million for the
Water and Sewer
Utility.
Today, we are allocating an additional $1 million of Gas Tax Funds to street
resurfacing, which will bring the City’s overall Capital Budget to $47 million, with
a net cost to the City of $26 million, after Federal and Provincial partner funding.

In these times of economic and employment uncertainty, the City must be
prepared to play its part in getting the economy up and running again.
Understanding the importance of that role, the City is prepared to expedite its
Capital Program once the risk of COVID-19 is diminished.
Some of the highlights for this year’s Capital being:
 $15.4 Million Water and Sewer Utility Upgrades
 $5.9 Million Municipal Buildings
 $4.2 Million New Transit Buses
 $3.8 Million Major Street Works (Paving/Micro Surfacing)
 $3.8 Million Fire Services/Emergency Services
 $3.5 Million Parks and Recreation Upgrades
 $2.5 Million Storm Water Management
 $2.2 Million Sidewalks and Multi-Purpose Pathways
 $2 Million Fleet Management, including heavy equipment
 $900,000 Intersection/Pedestrian Signal Upgrades
 $400,000 Corporate Energy Upgrades
Highlights from the operational budget include: $14.9 million for Public Works;
$14.1 million for Police and Fire services; $3.9 million for Parks and Recreation;
$1.2 million for transit; $660,000 for economic development; and $500,000 for
community sustainability initiatives, among other investments.
Your Worship, as the City’s parking garages age they
require increased maintenance to ensure continual use,
and over the next few years we will be spending several
million dollars in which to do so. It is this reason that we
are having to increase the parking rates for both the
parkades and for on-street parking.
Beginning September 1, 2020, on-street hourly parking
fees will increase to $2/hour, including HST. Parkades
fees will increase to $1.75/hour (including HST). Money
collected through parking will be used to maintain and
invest in future capital requirements for the City’s three
parkades.

Last year, Your Worship, you will recall that the City hit an all time low with its
rental vacancy rate of 0.2 per cent. In response to that demand, the City created
an Affordable Housing Action Plan providing financial incentives for developers to
respond to those needs. Your Worship, I’m pleased to say the development
community has started to responded to those needs, and as a result, the City’s
current vacancy rates stand around 1.3 per cent, and there is more on the way.
As seen on the graph below, the City’s building construction in all sectors
continues to flourish, ending 2019 with $157.8 million in construction value, an
incredible 50 per cent increase over the 2018 season of $105 million, making it
the best construction season in the City’s history.

So, your Worship, as we transcend to the new norm, we will have to work with all
stakeholders to get the economy up and running. In the interim, I encourage all
residents to do their part in helping us move the City forward. One idea that has
come to fruition is a Local Goods Guide sponsored by Discover Charlottetown,
which highlights local businesses that are still operating and how we can support
them while obtaining essentials.
The Local Goods Guide is a great, easy to look at resource for all the ways we
can all support local business during this challenging and unique time for all of
us. The guide is updated every Tuesday. You can find it online through Discover
Charlottetown’s website, and we thank them for putting that together.

At this time, please allow me to paraphrase a quote from Federation of Canadian
Municipalities President Bill Karsten:
What’s emerging from this pandemic is the best in community resilience
and compassion. We see it in the doctors and nurses and support staff
who risk their lives to save others. We see it in the bus drivers, garbage
collectors and our many civic workers who keep vital municipal services
going. We see it in everyone who picks up groceries for a senior, offers
words of encouragement to a friend, and does their part by just staying
home.
Your Worship and Members of Council, during these challenging times, I want to
take this opportunity to also thank Dr. Heather Morrison, Dr. Trevor Jain, Marion
Dowling and their medical staff, as well as Premier Dennis King and the Province
of PEI for continual leadership, guidance and advice in keeping Islanders safe.
In Closing…
As I’ve said many times before, developing and implementing the City’s budget is
not done in isolation by any one individual, it is done by many; it takes teamwork
and co-operation at all levels to make it work. In particular, I’d like to thank the
Finance, Audit and Tendering Committee members, Your Worship, and
Councillors Mike Duffy, Greg Rivard, and Kevin Ramsay for your commitment
and input during budget process.
I’d also like to thank our CAO Peter Kelly as well as our Finance Manager Mark
Lanigan and his team for their continual hard work, dedication and long hours.
I would be remiss if I didn’t also thank each and every member of City Council,
our Senior Management Team, and all City staff for working persistently every
day to provide municipal services that our residents continue to value, deserve
and expect. To all, thank you.
