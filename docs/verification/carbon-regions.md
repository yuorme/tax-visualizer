### NWT cash and heating

The first COLO payments were half of the $104 adult/$120 child entitlement in October 2019 and April 2020.
Subsequent benefit years pay quarterly in July, October, January and April. The annual adult/child amounts were
156/180 in 2020–21, 208/240 in 2021–22 and 260/300 in 2022–23. Zones begin with July 2023 payments.

Zone A includes the reference city Yellowknife. Annual 2023–24 amounts are A 441/493, B 483/535, C 558/610;
annual 2024–25 amounts are A 441/505, B 451/515, C 470/534. Each middle calendar year contains half of each
overlapping benefit year. The last payment is April 2025. Use the final annual report and the July 2023 change
sheet, not the older announcement that proposed different April 2023 amounts.

Heating fuel had a 100% point-of-sale rebate from September 2019 through March 2023. All heating fuels were
charged from April 2023 through March 2024. From April 2024, heating diesel alone again had a 100% rebate;
propane and natural gas remained charged until April 2025. Generator fuel was rebated throughout.
See [NWT report][nt-report] and [2023 changes][nt-change].

### Nunavut cash and heating

Nunavut replaced its 50% fuel rebate with the automatic Nunavut Carbon Credit in 2023. The 2023–24 annual
amount was $308 per person, including children. The July instalment was delayed until September 5, 2023;
the second was October 5. January and April 2024 each paid $77. From July 2024 the annual amount became
$100, or $25 quarterly. Thus calendar totals per person are 0 for 2019–22, 154 for 2023, 204 for 2024 and
50 for 2025. Final payment: April 4, 2025.

The current program page mixes old September timing with newer amounts. Prefer the contemporaneous
[October 2023 Hansard][nu-hansard], [July 2024 release][nu-2024] and [CRA program dates][nu-cra].
Do not interpret the delayed first payment as two quarters paid in September.

The 50% point-of-sale discount also covered heating diesel before April 2023. Full heating-oil charges
apply April 1–November 8, 2023, followed by the federal heating-oil exemption from November 9.
The separate 2023 $1,000 homeowner fuel relief required an application and April 1 homeownership.
It is excluded as extraordinary housing/inflation support, by the orchestrator's explicit scope decision.

## Alberta provincial levy and rebate in 2019

The levy was .0673 gasoline, .0803 diesel, .0462 propane and $1.517/GJ natural gas through May 29.
It was zero from May 30 until the federal charge began January 2020. The January and April provincial
rebates were retained in full, including the period after May 30; the July payment was cancelled.

The annual rebate formula is 300 + 150 for a spouse or a single parent's first child + 45 for each remaining
child, capped at four such children. Reduce by 2.67% of income above 47,500 for a childless single person,
or 4% above 95,000 for a family. Annual entitlement below $100 is zero, not topped up to $100.
Each January/April payment is one quarter of eligible annual entitlement. The generic current carbon
payment program lacks the child cap, two reduction rates and minimum-entitlement cutoff; a new rule is needed.
Sources: [2017 fiscal plan][ab-budget], [Bill 20 section 35.3][ab-law], [May 2019 Hansard][ab-end].

PE did not increase its levy in April 2021. PE furnace oil and propane were exempt under the provincial
system. NL heating oil and propane were likewise exempt. Ordinary gasoline-tax reductions accompanying
these levies belong in the ordinary fuel-tax schedule; they must not also be subtracted as carbon rebates.
No separate automatic provincial carbon cash payment has been identified for NB or NL.

PE's July 2022 payment included a separately identified carbon amount up to $140 per family. Published
examples give $140 at 45,000 income, $115 at 55,000, and $65 at 63,000. These support a .005 taper above
50,000; the exact legislative formula is being checked. The accompanying $150-per-adult inflation support
and January 2023 extraordinary support are excluded. Do not include the ordinary PE sales tax credit twice.
