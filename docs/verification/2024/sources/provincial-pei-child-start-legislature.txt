MLA Hamid: How many applications were received last year for childcare and how much
was spent?
In 2024/25, the Employment Support and Income Assistance program made 2,787 payments to
income assistance clients to cover childcare costs. This amounted to a total payment of
$776,140.
We cannot accurately identify the total number of applications received by the department from
individuals requesting financial assistance with childcare payments. The department’s case
management system only captures approved applications for assistance with special needs,
such as childcare.
MLA Hansen: What is the number of families considered homeless that the department
works with?
OSD defines “family” as single individuals with children, or couples with children.
As of May 16, 2025, there were 46 families in HRM who were considered homeless whom the
department was supporting through its Shelter Diversion Support Program. 174 total individuals
(including 80 children) were residing in hotels, most of whom were members of the 46 families.
Outside of HRM, there were two families considered homeless, with a total of four children and
three adults residing in hotels.
Hon. MLA Rankin: How does Nova Scotia compare to other provinces in the country
regarding income assistance?
The department prepared the following document to understand how Nova Scotia compares to
other provinces regarding income assistance rates.
Comparison of Nova Scotia’s Income Assistance Rates to Other Atlantic Provinces
• NS, NB, PEI and NL’s income assistance (IA) rate structures vary based on household
composition, accommodation type, employability of a client and other factors.
• NS and NB have basic needs and shelter costs combined into one rate, while PEI and NL
calculate basic needs and shelter costs separately.
o Prior to NS’ implementation of the Standard Household Rate in January 2020, NS’ IA
rates had the same structure as PEI and NL.
• NS and NB are the only Atlantic provinces that index IA rates based on the Consumer Price
Index.
• PEI has the highest IA rates in Atlantic Canada.

Additional Assistance
• The Prince Edward Island Child Benefit (PEICB) may provide a monthly benefit of $30 per
child to recipients. Payments are combined with the Canada Child Benefit (CCB). This is a
new benefit with the first payments in January 2025.
• Recipients may qualify for federal child benefits and other federal and provincial tax credits
and/or benefits.
• Persons with a disability are eligible for the AccessAbility Support program which provides
an Assured Income benefit. Funding is based on a capability assessment and ranges from
$0 (non-funded supports) to $4,000 monthly.
• All social assistance recipients in PEI are eligible for a Communication Rate that provides an
additional $40 –
$60 per month for phone/internet, however this is not included in basic needs rates or
shelter rates.

THE URGENT NEED TO SUBSTANTIALLY ADDRESS POVERTY IN NOVA SCOTIA (Submission to your Committee)
Dear Mrs. Corkum-Greek,
You are no doubt keenly aware of the high level of poverty in Lunenburg, and the province of Nova Scotia. Those of us
who are privileged with economic comfort have a moral obligation to take action to improve the lives of those who
struggle day to day with poverty.
As a reminder, some of the effects of poverty have been shown to be:
• Poor brain development on children not receiving adequate nutrition (which leads to a host of other societal
issues later in life)
• Poor health- often related to being unable to afford prescriptions. Many parents have to make the difficult
choices of “Food or Rent”, “Rent or Utilities”, “Food for Me, or Food for my Children”.
• Increased demand on social programs
• Low self-esteem and constant stress. Both of which can lead to addition, crimes of desperation, and increased
demand on the health care system
Mrs. Corkum-Greek, I urge you and your government to show increased compassion, and take immediate action please
to help our poor- many of whom are the working poor (40%). Some possible actions include:
1. Support to Bill-41. A test of the Basic Income Guarantee will provide the government a sound foundation on
which to build longer-term policy decisions.
2. Apply pressure to the Federal Government to accelerate delivery of the “Poverty Reduction Strategy”
3. Increase social assistance rates, substantially. Make the entire system less punitive.
4. Fast-track funding to municipalities in support of Affordable Housing plans and projects.
5. Remove the provincial portion of HST on everyday essentials such as bathroom products, feminine hygiene
products, dental care products, and gasoline.
6. Increase funding to Food Banks so that far more proteins can be provided. This might require funding additional
refrigeration at a local level. I also see potential for additional incentives to food producers in this system.
I thank you and your government for your anticipated attention to this desperate situation.
Sincerely,
