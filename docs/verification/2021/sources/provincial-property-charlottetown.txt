Your Worship and Members of Council,
We can readily agree that 2020 was a year like no other. Together, we
faced the global pandemic not only with trepidation, but also with honesty,
integrity, and empathy. Neighbours helped neighbours, Federal and
Provincial Governments stepped forward and responded to the needs of
our citizens and the business community, while non-profit agencies filled in
the gaps.
We should also, obviously, recognize the valiant efforts of Dr Heather
Morrison and her team at CPHO, as well as Premier Dennis King. Their
guidance during this time of adversity has been widely noted and
appreciated. Today, we collectively benefit from their foresight,
compassion, and leadership.
I am proud to say that today,
while restrictions are still in
place and public health
guidelines continue to be
followed, Prince Edward Island
is leading the charge in terms
of vaccinations per capita, and
Islanders continue to
demonstrate a profound and
admirable care for one another.

Your worship and Members of Council, it is also important that we take a
moment to acknowledge our staff who worked tirelessly over this last year
to ensure the City’s services continued through such challenging times. We
are certainly fortunate to have such a group of dedicated professionals who
willingly stepped forward, without hesitation, to serve our residents.
The pandemic has had a tremendous ripple effect on every part of our
lives, and it has become abundantly clear how much trust is placed upon
the decisions made here in these Council Chambers to move our City
forward in a direction that we can all be proud of. COVID-19 may have
changed many things about the way we live, work, learn, and play – but it
has not shaken Council’s unwavering dedication in our efforts to create
effective financial strategies, develop sustainable and long-term financial
plans, as well as maintain effective operational procedures.
On April 2nd of last year, our
municipal budget speech was
delivered by Councillor Terry
Bernard in a time of great unease;
in a near empty chambers with
most of us being at home and
communicating via Web-Ex,
certainly out of the norm. This year
we are fortunate to be back, in near
normal conditions, while respecting
social distancing and CPHO guidelines.

Public Input
This year’s budget process did not enable Council and Staff to hold an in-
person pre-budget Open House at City Hall, which is a key opportunity for
residents to ask questions and discuss the budget. Due to Covid -19, the
process was moved online. We are grateful for the residents who show
their commitment to their municipality when they voice their opinion on the
budget, and we hope that they continue doing so.
The City has also met with various stakeholders within the business
community to hear their views and concerns which have, in part, been
reflected within this budget. It has by no means been an easy year for our
business community. Despite everything, business-owners have risen to
the challenge with innovative strategies as well as pivots that drove
business to local operators, and on behalf of Council, I want to thank them
for their tenacity and innovation.
Budget Synopsis
The COVID-19 pandemic has shown us that charting a path forward can be
difficult, considering the level of economic uncertainty we currently face. A
municipal budget is continually in flux, it is a living document that provides
insight into a City’s priorities and areas of focus.
With this budget, we hope to offer some degree of certainty that the level of
service that our residents rely on will be maintained or in some cases
enhanced. The City will, of course, continue to face budgetary pressures
and revenue challenges in the coming year.

This year, Your Worship, we are pleased to announce to residents that
the City of Charlottetown will not increase the residential or
commercial municipal tax rate, as the City’s $61 million operating
budget is balanced. The Water and Sewer Utility rates will also not be
increasing. The Utilities’ $13.5 million dollar budget will also remain
balanced.
Earlier this month, Council approved its 2021/22 Capital Budget, with a
gross expenditure of $80.1 million: $68.6 million for the City and $11.6
million for the Water and Sewer Utility. This amount includes partner
funding of $22.9 million for the City and $7.4 million in partner funding for
the Utility.
The focus of the City’s 2021/22 Capital Budget was to inject capital that will
sustain infrastructure, create work opportunities, and improve the quality of
life of our citizens.

Some of the highlights for this year’s Capital budget include: A new fire
station as well as continued upgrades to our fire equipment; the
replacement of Simmons Sports Complex; investments at Bell Aliant
Centre and Eastlink Centre in preparation for the 2023 Canada Winter
Games; an investment in energy efficient upgrades of municipal buildings
to reduce their carbon footprint; a new Annex building on MacAleer Drive;
major additions to the City’s Active Transportation infrastructure; as well as
continued upgrades to the City’s wastewater treatment facility.
Highlights for the operational budget include $15 million for Public Works,
which includes the expansion of service areas to support our growing city
infrastructure; $10.2 million for Police Services, including two new police
officers to enhance public safety; $4.2 million for Fire services, as we look
to the future of our new volunteer station; $4.1 million for Parks and
Recreation, which continues to grow with additions to city trails and

recreational facilities; $1.4 million for transit, which will assist in bringing our
ridership back to pre-pandemic levels; $2 million for economic
development, tourism, arts, and culture, which will continue with strategic
investments to help our business community, while working within CPHO
guidelines to ensure our public events are safe; $1.8 million for Planning
and development, which includes updating the City’s official plan; $518,000
for community sustainability initiatives promoting a sustainable City
lifestyle; and continued investment in Information Technology systems to
meet the new normal for business and citizen engagement.
We recognize that top of mind for many Islanders are issues surrounding
housing. In response to the record low vacancy rate of 0.2 per cent in 2018,
the City developed an Affordable Housing Action Plan. Through the
combined efforts of C.H.M.C., the Provincial Government, the City, as well
as the development community, 167 affordable units were added to
Charlottetown in 2020, and the vacancy rate in October 2020 has increased
to 2.7%, up from 1.3% the year previous.

The City’s building construction in all sectors has seen unprecedented and
record-breaking growth. Building permit activity increased by 22 million
dollars from December 2019 to the end of December 2020. The City’s
construction value at the end of 2020 was $180 million, which is the highest
dollar value recorded in building permit activity to date.
In Conclusion
It must be noted that it can be exceptionally difficult to plan in times of
unexpected changes. This applies to a City budget, but on a smaller scale,
we acknowledge that there are many still struggling, and that there are
many families who continue to struggle to pay their rent or mortgage, and
to put food on the table. Though there is hope on the horizon, the pandemic
is not over; there is more to be done.
In looking back at this past year, one thing is clear: though there is much
that lies beyond the horizon of our influence, there is a need for a plan
which balances caution and optimism.

We would like to assure our residents that we are continually adjusting this
plan to be responsive to the ever-changing needs of our City. You hold us
to a high standard – and we assure you that we are striving to meet it.
From its earliest inception to its adoption, and through to its
implementation, a City budget is a team effort. I would like to acknowledge
my fellow Councillors whose input and advice has been instrumental in the
creation of this budget; Councillor Rivard, Councillor Duffy, Councillor
Ramsay, and Mayor Brown; thank you for your time and your efforts
throughout this process.
The final budget you see here relies on a tremendous amount of unseen
labor on behalf of many individuals. Our CAO, Peter Kelly, our Finance
Manager Mark Lanigan, and his team deserve our sincere gratitude. Thank
you, also, to every member of City Council, our Senior Management Team,
and all City staff for providing the municipal services that residents value,
deserve, and expect.
Your Worship, on behalf of the Finance, Audit and Tendering Committee,
as well as all the members of Council, I move for the adoption of the 2021-
2022 budget as presented.
