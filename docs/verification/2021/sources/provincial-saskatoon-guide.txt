Where Your Property Tax Dollars Go:
Education tax is collected to provide funding for the public and separate school divisions.
Municipal tax is invested in services we all rely on such as police/fire protection, road maintenance,
transit, snow management, parks, recreation and more.
Library tax is collected to fund the Saskatoon City Library programs and services.
Tax Questions?
Have other questions about property tax or assessment? Visit our website saskatoon.ca/propertytax or
call 306-975-2400. We're here to help.
PROPERTY TAX INFORMATION - Page 4
Explanation of Terms:
Assessed Value: The value of a property as of a given
base date determined by using legislative guidelines.
Deferred Tax: The tax amount to be paid at a later date
for approved applicants.(Seniors Property Tax Deferral
Program).
Taxable Assessment: The Province determines the
percentage of value (POV) that is taxable by a
municipality. The Taxable Assessment for a Residential
property is based on 80% of its Assessed Value; for
Commercial properties it's based on 85% of the
Assessed Value.
Lien Year: The year a tax lien was registered on the title
of the property.
Tax Rate: Mill Rate/1000 x Mill Rate Factor
(Mill Rate Factor is not applied to Education Tax).
Previous Balance: Payments, arrears, or other
charges added to the tax account prior to issuing the
tax notice. Arrears are related to a prior tax year and
subject to tax enforcement.
Tax Phase-in: The tax phase-in plan
(Commercial/Industrial properties) distributes the
change in property tax resulting from the 2021
reassessment over two years.
Other Charges (if applicable):
Tax Phase-In adjustment is shown for year one
for Commercial and Industrial properties only.
Special Charges are for encroachments and/or
awnings that extend onto City property.
Bid Levy is an additional tax on commercial
properties collected on behalf of each BID
(Business Improvement District).
Late Payment Penalty:
Payment received after June 30 of the current tax year will have a late payment penalty of 1.25% per month
applied.
If you pay your property tax after June 30 of the current tax year, call 306-975-2400 to confirm the amount due,
including penalties.
If your tax account is not paid in full by December 31 of the tax year, a 1.5% monthly penalty will be assessed
and the property becomes subject to The Tax Enforcement Act.
Seniors Tax Payment Assistance
Are you a low-income senior citizen age 65 or older who owns and resides in your own property in Saskatoon?
Consider applying for the Seniors Property Tax Deferral Program to defer payment of a portion of your
Municipal and Library Taxes for this year. To learn more about the deferral program and how to apply, visit
saskatoon.ca/propertytax or call 306-975-2400 (or 1-800-667-9944).

Education Tax Questions General Tax-Related Questions
Does the City still collect Education property
tax during the COVID-19 pandemic?
Yes. The City bills and remits Education
property tax to the Province and Separate
School Board.
I am a property owner without children. Am I
required to pay the Education property tax?
Yes. Regardless of your family status, The
Cities Act requires all property owners to pay
the full amount of property tax owing to the
three taxing authorities as shown on your tax
notice.
If I have questions about my Education
property tax, who should I contact?
Contact the Ministry of Government Relations
at 1-306-787-2653 or visit their website
saskatchewan.ca/residents/taxes-and-
investments/property-taxes.
What is municipal property tax used for?
Municipal property tax provides funding for
a wide variety of civic services and programs
Saskatoon residents rely on including Fire and
Police protection, Transit, Roadway & Snow
Maintenance, Utilities, Leisure Services, Waste
Disposal and more.
Can I appeal my property tax?
While property owners may appeal the
assessed value of their property, there is no
method to appeal your 2021 Property Tax
amount.
Did the reassessment of my property in 2021
change what I pay in property tax this year?
Only properties where the assessed value
increased or decreased more than the average
within its property tax class will see a tax
change due to the 2021 Reassessment.
View additional information at
saskatoon.ca/assessment.
