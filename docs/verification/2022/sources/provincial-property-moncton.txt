# Budget Information

The City of Moncton operates under capital, utility, and operating budgets.

## 2026 Budget

The proposed 2026 budget was presented to Moncton City Council on Nov. 4, 2025. Budget deliberations took place Nov. 4 and 5, 2025 and the final budget was approved Nov. 6, 2025.

- 2026 budget news release

The deliberations are recorded and available on the City of Moncton's YouTube channel.

## 2027 Budget

Budget deliberations for the proposed 2027 budget will take place on Nov. 3 and 4, 2026 and the final budget will be approved during a special public meeting on Nov. 5, 2026.

Deliberations and the special public meeting are open to the public and will be livestreamed on the City of Moncton’s YouTube channel.

## Municipal government

- City roads
- Police and fire services
- Drinking water
- Public transit
- Recreation
- Zoning (land use)
- Garbage, compost, and recycling

Moncton has three separate budgets:

General operating budget | Funds all municipal programs and services not covered by the utility and capital budgets, including parks, recreation programming, public transit, police and fire services, urban planning, and building inspection; the annual operating budget is around $236 million for 2026
Capital budget | Capital funds are used to build or rehabilitate city infrastructure, such as roads, storm sewer systems, buildings, and recreation facilities; the capital budget fluctuates more widely from year to year than the operating and utility budgets, but is generally between $50 and $60 million
Utility operating budget | Funds the provision of water and wastewater services; the annual utility budget is approximately $47.8 million for 2026

All budgets are multi-year budgets to help support long-term planning. They are reviewed and adjusted each year.

- Operating and utility budgets: three-year budgets
- Capital budget: five-year budget

The budget year follows the calendar year, from Jan. 1 to Dec. 31.

- The general operating budget is funded mainly through property taxes.
- The utility budget is funded through user fees (water and wastewater billing).
- The capital budget is supported through the operating budget, borrowing, and funding from other levels of government.

Each year the provincial government assesses the value of every property in the province. The property assessment reflects the market value of the property — the price the property would likely sell for on the open real estate market.

Your property tax bill is calculated as follows:

- Assessed value of your property/100 x (municipal tax rate)

For example, for a home in Moncton with a value of $379,603, the tax bill for 2026 is:

- $379,603 /100 x $1.3614 = $5,167 or $431/month

The total assessment base for the City can grow in two ways:

- The value of existing properties rises
- Additional homes, businesses, and other structures are built

The City’s goal is always to manage finances responsibly and to consider the long-term consequences of fiscal decisions. As a result, it considers its tax rate carefully each year.

Maintaining the tax rate has helped mitigate increases in tax bills for individual property owners who saw their assessments increase, while maintaining a budget that meets the needs of residents.

Municipal governments receive eight cents of every tax dollar you pay each year, including property taxes, income tax, HST, etc. In 2026, Moncton will receive $201.7 million in property taxes.

With those funds, the City of Moncton offers more than 85 services, including:

- builds, maintains, plows, and cleans city roads and sidewalks
- offers police and fire services
- provides drinking water
- operates public transit
- develops and delivers recreation activities
- oversees zoning (land use)
- provides waste collection, compost, and recycling services

Developing and implementing the budget is a year-round process. Planning for the following year’s budget begins early in the year and goes through to August. Budget development begins in earnest in the fall of each year.

### Timeline for the 2027-2029 budget

August to October 2026 | The City’s Finance Department collaborates with Moncton City Council and all City departments to develop a draft budget.
Nov. 2026 | An overview of the draft budget is presented to Moncton City Council and public budget deliberations are held in council chambers at Moncton City Hall. Each department is given the opportunity to present its budget to council.
Nov. 2026 | At the end of the budget deliberations, Moncton City Council votes on all motions presented during deliberations and the budget receives final approval.
Jan. 1, 2027 | The budget is implemented for 2027.
January/February 2027 | The provincial government grants final approval of the operating budget and the New Brunswick Municipal Borrowing Board approves any borrowing for the capital budget.
