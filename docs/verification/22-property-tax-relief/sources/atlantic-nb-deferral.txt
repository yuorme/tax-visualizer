# Property tax deferral program for seniors

This program helps eligible seniors in New Brunswick defer the annual increase in property taxes on their principal residence.

## On this page

## Overview

The property tax deferral program for seniors allows eligible New Brunswick seniors to defer the increase in their annual property taxes on their primary residence.

Deferred property tax and interest amounts form a lien against the property and are payable when the property is sold or transferred.

## Who can apply

You may qualify for this program if:

- At least one of the property owners listed on the deed is 65 or older
- The property is your principal residence
- The property is currently receiving the Provincial Residential Tax Credit
- Your property tax account is in good standing

## What it costs

Deferred property tax amounts are subject to annual interest that varies based on taxable household income:

Household income | Interest rate
Less than $124,178 | 3.949% per year
More than $124,178 | 8.949% per year

Annual interest rates are updated in line with the 10-year Provincial borrowing rate.

## What you'll get

If approved, you can defer the amount of your annual property tax increases over the “base year.”

The “base year” is the most recent of:

- The year before you turn 65
- The year in which you bought your property
- Or the year 2011

Example of deferred amount:

- 2020 property taxes: $2,100
- Base Year (2019) property taxes: $2,000
- Deferral for 2020: $100

The deferred amount plus interest will be shown on your annual property tax bill.

You can make full or partial payments on your deferred tax account at any time.

## Before you apply

You will need:

- Your Property Assessment Number (PAN)
- Social insurance number (SIN)
- Household income information from last years tax return(s)

## How to apply

You must apply by December 31 of the taxation year in which you want to start deferring your property tax increases.

You can apply online, by mail or in person.

## After you apply

If approved, your property tax account will be updated. Deferred taxes and interest amounts will be listed on your annual property tax bill.

Deferred taxes and interest will form a lien against your property. No payments are required while you are in the program.

### What happens if something changes

All deferred taxes and interest become due and payable to the province when the property is sold or transferred.

If the applicant passes away, the program can continue, provided the applicant is survived by a spouse or common-law partner who continues to own and reside in the property as their principal residence.
