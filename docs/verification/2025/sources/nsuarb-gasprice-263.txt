DECISION AND ORDER M12170
NOVA SCOTIA UTILITY AND REVIEW BOARD
IN THE MATTER OF THE PETROLEUM PRODUCTS PRICING ACT
- and -
IN THE MATTER OF PRESCRIBING PRICES for Petroleum Products under Section
14 of the Petroleum Products Pricing Act and Sections 16 to 19 of the Petroleum Products
Pricing Regulations
BEFORE: Roland A. Deveau, K.C., Vice Chair
Richard J. Melanson, LL.B., Member
DECISION AND ORDER
Under s. 16 of the Petroleum Products Pricing Regulations, the Board must prescribe a
benchmark price for specified petroleum products every Friday.
The benchmark price prescribed by the Board is the average of the average of the daily
high and low reported product prices (in Canadian cents) since the last benchmark price
adjustment for the petroleum product.
Under s. 17 and 18 of the Petroleum Products Pricing Regulations, the Board must also
prescribe retail mark-ups and calculate wholesale margins considering any factors the
Board finds relevant, including transportation adjustments. The Board last set wholesale
margins in its Decision, 2023 NSUARB 154; retail mark-ups in its Decision, 2021
NSUARB 50; and transportation adjustments in its Decision, 2016 NSUARB 168.
On December 23, 2024, the Board issued an Interim Order, 2024 NSUARB 215 (Matter
M11978), increasing the wholesale margin for self-service and full-service gasoline and
diesel, on an interim basis, by 3.00 cents per litre (cpI), up to a total of 13.84 cpI for
gasoline and 14.84 cpI for diesel, respectively, effective 12:01 a.m. on January 3, 2025.
Section 17 of the Petroleum Products Pricing Regulations also requires that the fixed
wholesale price for each type of petroleum product include all taxes (taxes imposed under
subsections 165(1) and 165(2) of the Excise Tax Act (Canada); carbon pricing, any fuel
charges payable to His Majesty in right of Canada, and a clean fuel adjustor amount set
by the Board under s. 17A to address the financial and administrative burdens associated
with the application of the Clean Fuel Regulations (Canada) or any other regulatory
instrument made under the Canadian Environmental Protection Act, 1999 (Canada) and

the Environmental Violations Administrative Monetary Penalties Act (Canada)). The
Board last revised and set the clean fuel adjustor amount in its Decision, 2024 NSUARB
111. The Government of Canada announced that it is eliminating the federal fuel charge,
commonly referred to as the “carbon tax”, effective Tuesday, April 1, 2025.
The Board issued an Order on September 12, 2022 [2022 NSUARB 140], approving an
adjustment mechanism to retail mark-ups for gasoline and diesel oil to account for the
impact of credit card fees charged to retailers. The Board also issued an Interim Order on
March 31, 2025 [2025 NSUARB 60], approving adjustments to the retail mark-ups for
gasoline and diesel oil to account for the inability of retailers to recover carbon taxes
already paid on remaining inventories held by retailers at the end of the day on March 31,
2025.
At each price setting, the Board may apply a forward averaging correction, based on
historical prices, to achieve revenue neutrality.
At appropriate times of the year, the Board may also require an additional margin for
winter blending of ultra-low-sulfur diesel oil.
This Order reduces the carbon tax to 0.00 cpl on both gasoline and diesel oil. The
Order also reduces the HST to 14%, as previously announced by the Government
of Nova Scotia. Both changes are effective April 1, 2025.
Based on the foregoing and other requirements in the Petroleum Products Pricing
Act and Regulations, the Board finds it appropriate to prescribe the credit card fee
retail mark-up adjustments in Table “B” and the federal fuel charge retail mark-up
adjustments in Table “D”. The benchmark prices and adjustments in Table “A” and
the Clean Fuel Adjustor in Table “C” remain unchanged from those prescribed by
the Board effective Friday, March 28, 2025. The Board prescribes the prices for
Petroleum Products in Schedule “A” effective on and after 12:01 A.M. Tuesday,
April 1, 2025:
